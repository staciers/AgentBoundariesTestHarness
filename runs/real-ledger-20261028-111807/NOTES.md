## Why the hidden-write arm differs (exploratory, not pre-registered)

Without a runtime, harm under a hidden write is a **race**. The agent's payment can be retried before or after
the original write becomes visible: before means a duplicate; after means the service rejects the retry because
the invoice is already paid. On both substrates the write becomes visible 6 s after the client's timeout.

The difference is entirely in retries that come 4.2–6 s after the timeout:

| Retry gap after the timeout | Simulated: harm | Real: harm |
|---|---|---|
| under 4.2 s | 82/84 | 89/89 |
| 4.2–6 s | **0/39** | **36/36** |
| 6 s or more | 0/21 | 0/23 |

**Cause: the simulator's service latency.** The simulated payment tool has a 1.8 s latency, and the fault
injector advances the simulated clock by it *before* executing the call (`abh/faults.py`, `invoke`). So a retry
issued 4.5 s after the timeout reaches the ledger check at 6.3 s and is rejected. On local Postgres the same call
executes in about 30 ms, inside the window, and pays twice. The effective invisibility window is about 4.2 s in
the simulator and 6 s on the real stack.

The models that differ are the ones whose decision time falls in that band:

| Model | Median retry gap (sim / real) | Harm (sim / real) |
|---|---|---|
| Opus 5.5 | 6.8 s / 5.9 s | 0/30 / 15/30 |
| GPT-6-Luna | 4.7 s / 5.2 s | 4/30 / 20/30 |
| Haiku 5.5 | 3.6 s / 3.0 s | 23/30 / 30/30 |
| DeepSeek V4.1-Flash, GLM-5.3-Flash | 1–3 s | 27–30 of 30 on both |
| GPT-6.1-Sol | never retried | 0 on both |

The sensitivity curve over all windows is in [latency_sensitivity.md](../../docs/latency/latency_sensitivity.md)
(exploratory). The real-stack retry timings, evaluated at 4.2 s, predict the simulator's result: 49% vs 46%
observed.

## What this means

1. **The paper's execution result holds on real infrastructure.**
   - The runtime prevented all harm (0/360 across both faults), as in the simulator.
   - Without it, harm was the same or higher.
   - The keys hazard appeared (T3).
   - Partial-commit behaviour matched the simulator almost exactly (27% vs 27%, 19% vs 18%).
2. **The simulator underestimated agent-side harm under hidden writes,** by 24 points pooled, because its
   service latency gave agents extra time. The difference isn't in agent behaviour; it is a timing parameter.
   Neither 1.8 s nor 30 ms is "the" latency: a deployed payment service is somewhere in between (Tier B).
3. **Per-model rates near a timing boundary are fragile.** Opus went from 0% to 50%, and GPT-6-Luna from 13% to
   67%, with no change in what they did, only in when the service checked. This qualifies the heterogeneity
   finding: Stage E's "Opus and the GPT models barely needed the runtime on the ledger" is partly an artifact of
   simulated latency. The runtime's guarantee doesn't depend on that timing; agent-side recovery does.
4. **Visibility lag alone did no harm** (0/360 on each substrate): agents trusted a successful response and
   didn't pay again within 9 s.

## Exploratory, also noted

- GPT-6.1-Sol never retried a payment after a timeout, on either substrate, which is why it was never harmed.
- Real episodes took 7–19 s (median wall clock), against 8–51 s simulated. The simulator's clock includes its
  30 s timeout and simulated latencies.
