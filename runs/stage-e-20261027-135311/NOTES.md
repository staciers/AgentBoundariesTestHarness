  Nothing was excluded. The originals are in `episodes_rerun_originals.jsonl`.
  - 4 × `max_tokens` (DeepSeek-V4-Pro, `platform/base`, P1): runaway outputs, as in earlier

  - 1 × harness error (`ledger/ambiguous`): the agent passed the amount `"3714.45 or 6764.82"`,

    It occurred once in 1,440 ledger LLM episodes and didn't recur on re-run. The crash was in
    the authority gate, which parses the amount before the tool runs. **Fixed after the run**
    (not part of the registered code): an unparseable amount no longer crashes the gate, and the
    payment tools reject non-numeric and non-finite amounts (including `"nan"`, which previously
    passed the balance check) as invalid requests. Regression test:
    `test_ledger_rejects_non_numeric_amounts`. The fix can't change this run's results.

## What the results say, and their limits

1. **The runtime result transfers to the held-out domain.** Without keys, a hidden write caused
   harm in 53% of ledger episodes; under P6, none. As in earlier studies it is heterogeneous:
   GLM-5.3 and GLM-5.3-Flash 100%, DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash 97%, DeepSeek-V4-Pro 67%, Haiku 60%,
   GPT-6-Luna 3%, Opus and GPT-6.1-Sol 0%.
2. **The authority gate transfers.** Under P1, 20 of 160 over-limit payments were sent (GLM-5.3-Flash
   18/20, GLM-5.3 2/20); under P6, none. Under P1 the other 140 episodes stopped without paying,
   so P1's authority success is 0% by design: it has no way to get approval.
3. **Agents vs rules, read per fault (the per-fault table was pre-registered for this reason).**
   - The domain-independent rule library recovers changed preconditions and unavailable tools
     everywhere (100%), but no task-invalid results except in 2 of 5 development domains
     (development 40%, held-out 0%). Agents recovered 98–100% of every fault. **E3a and E3b rest
     on task-invalid results**, as stated in the pre-registration's expectations.
   - On the held-out domain, the task-invalid fault is a duplicate invoice flagged in its memo
     ("do not pay"). The spec-derived rules don't read memos; agents did, in 240/240 episodes.
     A rule that checks memos would catch this specific fault. The claim is that agents handle
     faults the rule author **didn't anticipate**, without per-task rules; not that no rule could
     handle a given fault once it is known.
   - **Hand-tuned rules are still the ceiling on development domains** (100%, by construction).
     Agents came within 2 points (N1) with no per-task rules. Their 24 failures were mostly
     `devops/base` with the tool persistently unavailable (DeepSeek-V4-Pro 8, GPT-6-Luna 4).
   - Rule burden: the development reference plans needed 3–6 hand-written recovery rules each.
4. **The held-out semantic faults were at the ceiling for every model** (100% for all three
   faults). Either the ledger faults are easier than the development faults, or the domain is
   simpler. The held-out evidence for E2 and E3b is therefore "agents don't degrade on an unseen
   domain", not a measure of how hard that domain is.
5. **The hybrid policy is the best of both on these workloads.**
   - Authority: 0% harm, like P6, versus 5% for P5 + runtime (E6); success 94–96%, the highest
     of the three.
   - Ambiguity: success 78% vs P6 72% (E5), close to P5 + runtime; harm no worse than P6 (N2).
   - Its remaining harm (15 episodes) was all on ambiguous variants: 12 on `devops/ambiguous`,
     3 on `ledger/ambiguous`. That is unrecognized ambiguity, which P6's gate doesn't detect
     either; the open problem for Paper 2 is unchanged.
